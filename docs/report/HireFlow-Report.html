1 · Problem understanding

What the brief asks for

Organisations need one system to publish vacancies, collect applications, move candidates through recruitment stages, schedule interviews and record structured feedback. Every action must be traceable, and candidate information must be protected.

RequirementHow HireFlow covers it
Employer, Recruiter, Hiring Manager, Candidate rolesRole-based access control in middleware plus a single resource-level access module; hiring managers are scoped to their own jobs in SQL
Job postings & application formsJob editor with a status machine (draft → published ⇄ paused → closed) and custom questions of five types
Candidate profiles, resume upload, trackingCandidate portal, S3 resume storage, automatic resume parsing, application timeline
Pipeline with configurable stagesPer-vacancy stage list edited as data, Kanban board, approval gates
Interview scheduling & evaluation formsPanel scheduling with double-booking checks, 1–5 scorecards per job criterion
Templates & notificationsEditable email templates, sandbox email provider (Mailpit), in-app notifications
Analytics & audit historyTime-to-stage, sources, vacancy status, conversion funnel; append-only audit log
USP: configurable hiring workflowStages, notifications and approvals per vacancy, without code changes
USP: candidate data governanceConsent capture, role-limited access, retention, export and deletion workflows
2 · Solution overview

Who uses HireFlow

Candidate

Signs up with consent, uploads a resume that is parsed automatically, sees how well they match every role, applies, follows each stage on a timeline, and can export or delete their data.

Recruiter

Creates vacancies and their pipelines, screens and moves candidates on a Kanban board, schedules interviews, sends templated emails and reads analytics.

Hiring manager

Sees only the vacancies they own, sits on interview panels, submits scorecards, and approves stage moves (such as offers) that are gated to them.

Employer

Runs the organisation: team and roles, final approvals, retention policy, deletion requests and the full audit trail.

3 · Architecture & technology choices

How the system is put together

React SPA→ /api/v1 (cookie) →Express API→PostgreSQL·S3 storage·Mailpit

The browser talks only to the API. The API owns all business rules and writes to PostgreSQL through Prisma. Resume files go to S3-compatible storage and are read through short-lived presigned links, so files never stream through the API. Emails are rendered from templates and handed to an SMTP sandbox without blocking the request. A nightly job runs the data-retention sweep.

Backend design rules

  • Thin routes: each one parses its input, authorizes, calls lib/, writes to the audit log and responds.
  • One way to change a stage: moveApplication(). It checks that the stage belongs to the job, enforces the approval gate, uses an optimistic lock, writes the stage history and sends the notification.
  • One place for permissions: lib/access.ts, which prevents users from opening other people's records by changing an ID.
  • Analytics from history: StageTransition is append-only and every report is a single SQL aggregate.
4 · How it was built

Phases & bottleneck analysis

Before writing code, we studied similar open-source applicant trackers (notably OpenCATS) and typical PERN job portals for known failure points, and designed against them from day one.

Bottleneck seen elsewhereFix built into HireFlow
Candidate list queries examining 300k+ rows for 15 results (10–18 s page loads at 50k candidates)Lean single-query Kanban data, composite indexes, pagination
Hard-coded pipeline stagesStages and approval gates per vacancy, stored as data
Analytics computed in application memorySQL aggregates over the append-only stage history
Email sending blocking the requestQueued email log, sent without awaiting, status updated afterwards
Resumes streamed through the server5 MB MIME-checked upload to S3; 5-minute presigned downloads
Users reading others' records by changing an ID (IDOR)Central access module plus tests that prove the 403s
Two recruiters moving the same cardOptimistic lock: a stale move returns 409 "refresh and retry"
Interviewer double-bookingOverlap check on every schedule and reschedule
Personal data kept foreverConsent records, retention sweep, anonymization

Build phases

  1. Scaffold: repository, Docker services (Postgres, S3 storage, mail sandbox), Prisma schema and migrations.
  2. Auth & RBAC: login, cookies, roles, access module, audit trail.
  3. Domain APIs: jobs, candidate portal, pipeline engine, interviews, email, reports, governance.
  4. Frontend: every screen, built in parallel by a specialised sub-agent against the finished API.
  5. Quality: 67 backend tests and the API and database docs, each produced by its own sub-agent. Their reviews caught real bugs (a missing auth check, unvalidated query parameters, data requests not scoped to the organisation), which were fixed.
  6. Product polish: design system, landing page, full-screen auth, dark theme, animations, resume parsing, real-resume demo data.
5 · End-to-end workflow

From vacancy to hire

Recruiter publishes job→Candidate signs up + uploads resume→Applies→Screening→Interview + scorecards→Offer approval→Hired
  1. Recruiter creates a vacancy (fields, skills, questions, and a pipeline from a template or the default) and publishes it. Only published jobs appear publicly and accept applications.
  2. Candidate signs up. Privacy consent is required and is stored with its policy version and IP address. They upload a PDF resume; the server reads it and fills in skills, phone, city, LinkedIn and headline.
  3. The job board ranks every role by skill match. The candidate answers the role's questions and applies; an "application received" email goes to the sandbox inbox.
  4. Recruiter drags the card to Screening. The move is recorded as a stage transition and in the audit log, and a stage-update email is sent if that stage has notifications on.
  5. Recruiter schedules an interview with a panel. A clash with an existing interview is refused with the busy person's name. The candidate is emailed the time and place.
  6. Panel members submit scorecards rated 1–5 per job criterion, with a recommendation.
  7. The recruiter moves the candidate to Offer, which is gated: the API answers 202 and creates a pending approval. The configured approver (hiring manager or employer) approves, the move completes, and the offer email is sent.
  8. Moving to Hired closes the application. Analytics update from the stage history.
  9. Later, the candidate can export all their data or request deletion. The employer approves, personal data is anonymized, files are deleted, and the hiring statistics stay correct.

Edge cases demonstrated

Edge caseBehaviour
Recruiter moves a candidate into an approval-gated stage202 Accepted, card shows "Approval pending", candidate stays put until the approver decides
Scheduling an interviewer who is already booked409 "Double booking: <name> already has an interview at that time"
Deleting a stage that still holds candidates409 with the number of candidates to move first
Candidate opens another candidate's application or a staff page403 Forbidden
Applying twice, or applying to a paused job409 / 400 with a clear message
6 · Page-by-page walkthrough

Public site

Landing page /

Purpose: the front door for first-time visitors. It explains what HireFlow is and lets each visitor choose a path: Find a job or I'm hiring.

What's on it: a hero with a product preview, live facts computed from the open roles (count, teams, locations), "One flow, two sides" for candidates and hiring teams, a four-step "How it works", three live job cards, and a closing call to action. Sections fade in as you scroll. Signed-in users skip this page and go straight to their workspace.

Dark theme

Every page supports light and dark themes through the sun/moon toggle. The choice is remembered, and first-time visitors get their device's setting. Dark mode is implemented by switching the colour variables, so all pages follow automatically. An automatic contrast check passed on 14 pages.

Job board /jobs

All published vacancies, with keyword search, type filters and a location filter built from the live jobs. Visitors who aren't signed in see salary and role essentials and a tip inviting them to sign up to see match scores.

Job detail & application form /jobs/:id

The full description, requirements and skills. Candidates apply here: they answer the role's custom questions (all five types are supported), pick or upload a resume and choose how they heard about the role. Required answers, duplicate applications and paused jobs are all checked on the server.

Log in & sign up /login · /register

A full-screen split card with an illustrated sunset-forest panel. Log in has one-click demo accounts for every role. Sign up checks that the two passwords match and requires privacy consent, with optional talent-pool consent. Both are stored as consent records with their policy version.

On a phone

Every page works down to 375 px wide. On phones the auth card puts the form on top with the forest as a band along the bottom, and fits the screen exactly. The staff app's navigation moves into a slide-out menu.

Candidate experience

Job match /jobs as a candidate

For signed-in candidates the board becomes "Your job match": every role shows a ring with the share of its required skills found on the candidate's profile, the best fits come first, and skills the candidate already has are shown as dark chips.

My applications /portal

Each application shows a candidate-friendly status (internal stage names are kept internal, and rejections read "Not selected"), a dated stage timeline, upcoming interviews with time and place, and a withdraw button.

Profile & resumes /portal/profile

Candidates edit only the fields they're allowed to edit; the API rejects anything else. Uploading a PDF resume runs the parser: detected skills are added and empty contact fields are filled in, without overwriting anything the candidate typed. Resumes open through 5-minute presigned links.

Privacy & data /portal/privacy

Candidates can grant or withdraw talent-pool and marketing consent (history is kept), download everything held about them as JSON, and request deletion. They can see each request's status as the employer processes it.

Hiring team

Dashboard: hiring pulse /dashboard

Three nested half-discs show applied → interviewed → hired, sized by area. Panels on the right open to show talent overview, sources and vacancy status. Below, a vacancy filter and swipeable stage cards show, for each stage, the share of candidates who reached it, the average days to get there, and how many haven't yet. Hiring managers see only their own vacancies.

Left: the dashboard in dark mode. Right: the vacancies list.

Vacancies /jobs-admin

Every vacancy with its status and applicant count. Status buttons show only the legal next states (for example, a closed job can't be republished), and each row links to its pipeline and workflow editor.

Creating a job

Title, department, location, employment type, hiring manager, salary range (min ≤ max is validated), description, requirements, skills, custom application questions, and a pipeline template. New jobs start as drafts.

Pipeline Kanban /jobs-admin/:id/pipeline

One column per stage of this vacancy. Cards show the candidate, their source, a skill-match ring and badges (interviews, approval pending), sorted best match first. Drag-and-drop or "Move to…" calls the pipeline engine; gated stages answer "Sent for approval".

Workflow editor ⭐ /jobs-admin/:id/workflow

The first USP. Recruiters rename, reorder, add and remove stages; set each stage's category (active, hired or rejected); choose whether entering it emails the candidate and with which template; and set whether it needs approval and from which role. Scorecard criteria are edited here too, and any pipeline can be saved as a reusable template. Safety rules are enforced by the API: a rejected stage is required, names must be unique, and a stage that still holds candidates can't be deleted.

Candidate detail /applications/:id

Everything about one application: profile and resume (each view is audited), answers, interviews with panels and scorecards (with an average per criterion), internal notes, approvals, the complete stage history with who moved the candidate and when, and the email log. From here recruiters can move or reject the candidate, schedule an interview or send a templated email.

My interviews & scorecards /interviews

Each interviewer's queue. Candidate email and phone are masked (role-limited access). Scorecards require a 1–5 rating for every criterion of the job plus a recommendation, and only assigned panel members can submit them.

Approvals /approvals

Pending stage moves that need a sign-off. The employer can approve anything; otherwise only the configured approver role can (and a hiring manager only on their own jobs). Approving completes the move and sends the stage's email; denying leaves the candidate where they are.

Email templates & log /templates

Editable templates with placeholders such as {{candidateName}}, {{jobTitle}} and {{interviewDate}}. Placeholder values are HTML-escaped. Every send is logged as queued, then sent or failed, and all mail lands in the Mailpit sandbox, never in a real inbox.

Audit log /audit

An append-only record of every write: who did it, in which role, what changed (before/after), on which record, from which IP and when. It's filterable by entity and action and paginated.

Data governance ⭐ /governance

The second USP. The employer sets the retention period, can run the retention sweep on demand (it also runs nightly), and reviews deletion requests. Approving a request anonymizes the candidate: name and email are replaced, contact fields cleared, answers and notes deleted, resume files removed from storage. Application rows and stage history are kept, so analytics remain correct.

Team /users

The employer creates staff accounts, changes roles and deactivates people; deactivated users can no longer log in. Right: the dashboard on a phone.

API documentation :4000/api/docs

An OpenAPI 3 specification for all 62 operations, served by Swagger UI, with request schemas, the roles allowed per endpoint, error formats and examples (including the 202 approval-pending and 409 double-booking responses). A human-readable version with curl examples is in docs/API.md.

7 · USPs in depth

Configurable hiring workflow

  • Every vacancy owns its ordered JobStage rows: name, order, category, notify flag, email template, requires-approval flag and approver role.
  • Edits are diffed against the current stages inside a transaction. Stages still holding candidates can't be removed, and the rules (a rejected stage, unique names, an approver for every gate) are validated.
  • The pipeline engine reads these rows at run time, so a new process (for example "Online Test → Tech Interview → Employer-approved Offer" for internships) needs no code.
  • Pipelines can be saved as templates and chosen when creating a job.

Candidate data governance

  • Consent: data-processing consent at sign-up, per-application consent, and optional talent-pool and marketing consent, each with policy version, IP and timestamp; withdrawals are kept in the history.
  • Role-limited access: interviewers see masked contact details, hiring managers see only their jobs, and every staff resume view is audited.
  • Retention: candidates whose every application in the organisation is rejected or withdrawn and older than the retention period are anonymized automatically.
  • Export & deletion: self-service JSON export, and deletion requests with employer review, as described on the governance page.

9 · Resume parsing & real-resume demo data

Reading resumes on the server

PDF text is extracted with unpdf. Heuristics then find the email, phone, LinkedIn URL, city and headline, and match against a list of about 40 skills to produce canonical skill tags that line up with job requirements. Nothing is sent to an outside service.

A bulk importer and a resume-based seed mode turned a batch of 26 real student resumes into candidates: each was parsed, stored, applied to the best-fitting open role, and placed in that role's pipeline by skill match (hired, offer awaiting approval, interviewing with scorecards, assessment, rejected). 24 of 26 parsed fully; 2 scanned PDFs without a text layer were imported with name and file only. The real resumes are gitignored and never published; all screenshots in this report use generated demo data.

10 · Security considerations

Defence in depth

  • Authentication: bcrypt hashes; JWT in an httpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie with 8-hour expiry; login rate-limited; deactivated users rejected.
  • Authorization: role middleware plus resource checks in one module; hiring-manager scope applied inside SQL; tests prove the 403s.
  • Input: Zod on every body and query; strict candidate profile schema; validation errors returned as 400s with per-field details.
  • Files: MIME allow-list and 5 MB limit; private bucket; 5-minute presigned reads; resume views audited.
  • Transport & headers: helmet, CORS allow-list, 1 MB JSON limit.
  • Data: HTML-escaped email templates, masked PII for interviewers, anonymization on deletion, no secrets in Git.
